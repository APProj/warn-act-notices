Which should you use?

Journalists / researchers: the free tier here covers most needs (2024–present, normalized, citable, CC BY); for deeper custom scraping, Big Local News' warn-scraper is the gold-standard DIY tool. Recruiters and sales-intelligence teams who only care about certain employers or states: WARN Watch ($49/year) checks your list on every refresh and gives you a private alert page + RSS. Analysts who need decades of history: the full 1988–present archive (45,760 notices) is $199 one-time.

FAQ

Is there a free WARN notice database?

Yes. The WARN Feed free tier is a normalized, no-login dataset of 9,428 WARN notices across 39 states (2024–present), published as CSV and JSON on GitHub with daily commits, under CC BY 4.0. It carries no delay: rows are published on the refresh that first sees them.

Does the federal government publish a national WARN database?

No. The WARN Act is administered state-by-state: each state labor department publishes its own notice list in its own format, and the US DOL links out to those state pages rather than hosting a consolidated database.

What does WARN data cost from commercial providers?

Checked 2026-09-03: WARNTracker's public pricing lists a $250/month subscription and a $2,000 one-time historical CSV; Intellizence sells an enterprise layoff-signals API with pricing on request. WARN Feed's per-customer WARN Watch is $49/year and its full 1988–present archive (45,760 notices) is $199 one-time.

What is the cheapest way to get the whole WARN archive?

Checked 2026-09-03, the paid options are priced per month: WARN Firehose charges $199/month for full history (its $49/month tier gives one year), WARNTracker lists $250/month or a $2,000 one-time CSV, and Intellizence is enterprise-quoted. WARN Feed sells the same kind of thing once rather than monthly: the full 1988–present archive (45,760 notices) is $199 one time, and the free tier (9,428 notices, 39 states) needs no account at all. If you also want H-1B, SEC and bankruptcy data through one API, Firehose is the better buy and we would tell you so.

How fresh is WARN notice data?

States post notices on their own schedules — some daily, some weekly. This pipeline re-scrapes every state source multiple times a day and publishes every new row on the refresh that first sees it — no hold.
